On building a portfolio website

Choosing simplicity and restraint, and what that says about how I think about design.

The job of a portfolio

I believe a portfolio's job is to communicate clearly, and get out of the way. Instead of losing myself in complexity -- more animation, more tooling, more spectacle -- I wanted this site to reflect restraint.

As Blaise Pascal said, "I have only made this letter longer because I have not had the time to make it shorter." Thoughtful design almost always involves removing something, not adding it.

The job of a builder

I believe in getting the foundation right. It's easy to reach for a framework that comes with pre-built components and features. But I want to get closer to the basics, and learn what it takes to walk before I use these tools to fly.

Moreover, a portfolio has maybe five pages, a stylesheet, and a small script file. I want to build something simple and small that can last, instead of maintaining a dependency that can break anytime.

(Of course, with a generous dash of support from Cursor and Claude.)

An opening, not a summary

The work here shows shipped outcomes, but some of what I find most rewarding — building trust across teams, developing frameworks, mentoring designers through ambiguous problems — lives outside case studies. I think of this site as an opening to a conversation, not a summary of one.

It's not the full range of what I can make. I'd love to show you more in conversation.
